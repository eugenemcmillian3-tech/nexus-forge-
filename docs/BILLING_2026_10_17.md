# Paid subscriptions and prepaid usage — 17 October 2026 UTC

## Implemented

| Purchase | USD price | Included usage credit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core monthly | $49/month | $10 per paid billing period |
| Pro monthly | $149/month | $35 per paid billing period |
| Business monthly | $399/month | $100 per paid billing period |
| Prepaid usage | $10, $25, $50 or $100 once | The purchased amount |

No free plan, free trial, free credit grant or automatic prepaid refill exists. Prices are catalog-controlled on the server; checkout does not accept client prices or quantities. Products are seeded in the database on initial checkout. The public pricing view can show the same server catalog definitions before the first purchase.

Subscriptions currently fund one purchasing operator's workspace. The earlier proposed 3/10-user allocations are not sold or advertised as implemented: shared-seat allocation and pooled team billing remain separate work. Enterprise is not offered through self-service checkout. External hosting, domains and separately billed services are excluded.

## Routes and workflow

- /pricing: public plan and metering explanation, both payment models, explicit test-mode notice.
- /checkout: authenticated dedicated embedded payment form. Completion alone never grants credit.
- /billing: authenticated private balances, holds, charges, cost basis, payment receipts, invoice links and billing-management portal.
- Account entry explains paid-only access and links pricing. Billing is outside the sixteen-page feature guide.

Monthly checkout uses recurring inline price data; prepaid checkout uses a one-time inline price with invoice creation. There are no discounts or trials. Persisted checkout intents, provider idempotency keys and open-subscription checkout serialization limit duplicate sessions. Paid invoice periods grant monthly allowance once per period; unused monthly allowance expires rather than becoming prepaid value. Payment and invoice identities deduplicate credit fulfillment. Customer cancellation/payment state is refreshed from the provider to avoid blindly applying stale event payloads.

All balances, receipts, checkout intents and usage holds are isolated by payment mode and authenticated account. Webhooks use the two-layer HTTP/Node architecture, verify signatures and signed event mode, and retrieve canonical payment state. Refunds freeze the matching account for review. Existing mission/deployment approval controls remain in place.

## Metering and execution

Each model inference operation authorizes at most $1 from available usage credit before contacting a provider. Multi-agent and multi-stage workflows may authorize several operations. The authorization is disclosed on pricing; it is not an automatic $1 charge. Concurrent transactional reservations cannot spend the same available credit twice.

Settlement uses twice the reported provider dollar cost when available. Otherwise complete input/output token counts can use the exact-model standard rate card below. Charges round upward to whole cents and are capped at $1 per inference operation; excess provider cost is absorbed. Zero measured cost remains zero. The rate version and cost basis are retained. Free provider quotas, invoice discounts and negotiated rates are not represented by a standard token calculation.

Unknown models, missing token/cost evidence, incomplete reports or interrupted accounting do not become fabricated charges. Funds remain held, and unresolved cost review blocks further inference. Privileged internal review requires evidence, a bounded charge and an audit note; confirmed platform errors can receive an idempotent credit reversal. These controls cover direct model requests, named evaluation calls and recoverable runner stages. The separate provider health path requires a funded account with at least $1 available credit and transactional admission of at most one diagnostic batch per ten minutes, twenty per UTC day; those bounded diagnostic probes are included without a usage-credit deduction. Recovering a previously saved answer does not reserve or debit again.

Test credit can authorize controlled Testing inference only. The production backend rejects test-mode inference. Missing deployed payment configuration also blocks inference. Thus publishing this test setup does not enable unpaid production AI or real charges.
